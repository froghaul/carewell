Getting ready for a My Aged Care assessment (Support at Home)

Almost all government-funded help at home in Australia starts with one step: an aged care assessment through My Aged Care. Since November 2025 most ongoing help at home comes through the Support at Home program, which replaced Home Care Packages. Here is how to get the assessment started and how to make the most of it.

1. Apply (10 to 15 minutes)

  • Online at myagedcare.gov.au. You can apply for yourself or on behalf of Mum or Dad. Have their Medicare card ready.
  • By phone on 1800 200 422.
  • In person: book with an Aged Care Specialist Officer at a Services Australia centre on 1800 227 475.

Your answers decide how urgently the assessment happens, so describe a normal week honestly, including the bad days. An assessment organisation will then ring you (or the person you nominate) to set a time.

2. Gather what the assessor will ask for

  • Medicare card and one other ID (driver's licence, health care card, passport or DVA card)
  • Any referrals from the GP
  • Contact details for the GP and other health professionals
  • What help they already get, from family or services
  • Anything you've already read about services you want to ask about

3. Write down what a normal week looks like

The assessment usually happens at home, in person. It's easy to downplay things on the day ("we manage fine"). Beforehand, jot down what's getting harder: showering, meals, cleaning, shopping, getting to appointments, falls or near falls, and what family members are quietly doing. A few weeks of short daily notes make this much easier and more accurate.

4. Decide who will be there

A family member, friend or carer can be at the assessment, and can give the assessor information beforehand. You can also ask the assessor to register you as a supporter, so you can help with aged care discussions and paperwork later. Interpreters are free and can be requested at any time.

5. Questions worth asking the assessor

  • What services could help with the things we've described?
  • What's available locally, and what are the waiting times?
  • What support is there for me as the carer?
  • How do we contact you if we have questions afterwards?

6. After the assessment

If they're approved, a letter explains the services and funding. When the funding comes through, you choose a provider and agree the mix of services. You can ask for a reassessment if needs change. Under Support at Home you contribute to the cost of some services based on what you can afford, but not to clinical care such as nursing.

Need free, independent help with any of this? The National Aged Care Advocacy Line is 1800 700 600, and Carer Gateway (1800 422 737) supports you as the carer.
