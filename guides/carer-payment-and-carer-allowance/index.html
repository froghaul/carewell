Carer Payment and Carer Allowance: which one, and can you get both?

Many people looking after Mum or Dad don't realise they could get help from Centrelink, or assume it's one payment or the other. There are two main ones, they work differently, and you can sometimes get both.

Carer Allowance: the extra payment

  • For people giving daily care and attention at home (theirs or yours) to someone with a disability, a medical condition, or who is frail aged.
  • The person you care for needs care for at least 12 months, or has a terminal condition, and their treating doctor fills in a medical form.
  • No assets test. There is an income test: you and your partner's combined adjusted taxable income must be under $250,000 a year. There's no income test for the person you care for.
  • You can work full time and still get it. It's paid fortnightly; Services Australia lists the current rate.

Carer Payment: income support if caring stops you working full time

  • For people giving constant care, which Services Australia describes as roughly a normal working day, to someone who needs care for at least 6 months, or who is at the end of their life.
  • Care can be physical help, guidance (like shopping and taking the right medication) or supervision.
  • Income and assets tested for you, and the person you care for also has to be under income and assets limits.
  • You can work, study or volunteer: paid work or self-employment of up to 100 hours in a 4-week period is allowed. Study, training, volunteering and travel to work don't count towards the 100 hours. You must report work changes within 14 days.
  • If you sometimes go over 100 hours, you can use respite days (up to 63 a calendar year) to keep the payment.

Can you get both?

Yes, depending on your circumstances, and you can make one combined claim for both.

How to claim

  1. Check you're eligible on the Services Australia pages for each payment.
  2. Claim online through myGov (linked to Centrelink), or call the Centrelink disability and carers line.
  3. You'll be asked how you care for them, and their treating doctor will usually need to complete a medical form, so book a GP visit early.

A tip that helps every claim

Keep short notes of what you actually do each day: medicines, appointments, meals, nights up, falls. It makes the "how do you care for them?" questions much easier to answer honestly, and it's useful for the doctor's form too.

Carer Gateway (1800 422 737) can also help with respite, counselling and coaching for you.
