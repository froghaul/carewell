How to keep track of a parent's medicines

When a parent is on five, eight or a dozen medicines, the problem usually isn't remembering to take them — it's the packet that quietly runs out on a long weekend, or the script with no repeats left that nobody noticed. Here is a simple routine that stops that happening.

1. Make one list

Write every medicine down once: the name on the box, the strength, how many tablets per dose, how many doses a day, and what it's for. Include creams, puffers and anything from the chemist. Keep the list where the whole family can see it.

2. Count each packet once

For each medicine, count the tablets left and write the number with today's date. Then:

  • Tablets a day = tablets per dose × doses a day
  • Days left = tablets ÷ tablets a day
  • Runs out on = today + days left

Write the run-out date on the box itself. It's the single most useful thing you can do.

3. Reorder a week early

Aim to get the next box when about seven days are left. That covers weekends, public holidays and the pharmacy not having it in stock.

4. Watch the repeats, not just the tablets

A script with no repeats left needs a doctor's appointment before the pharmacy can help. Check how many repeats remain when you collect each box, and book the GP before the last one is used.

5. Log every pick-up

Each time a box is collected, note the date and how many tablets it added. Recount every month or two — spills, missed doses and spare packets add up.

6. Note anything the doctor pauses or stops

Write the date and who said so. Old packets of stopped medicines are a common cause of mix-ups; take them back to the pharmacy.

This is general organising advice, not medical advice. Always follow the instructions of the doctor and pharmacist.
